# Independent head-to-head benchmarks: TypeSafe AI Jev vs OpenAI Decisions API (gpt-6-luna), Oct–mid-Nov 2026

Scope note: "Jev" below means TypeSafe's hosted decision model (jev-1.13.0 in almost every test; 1.14 appears only on the Nov leaderboards). "Decisions" means OpenAI's Decisions API on `gpt-6-luna` (public beta 2026-10-06 per the jev-measured repo; DevDay announcement 2026-09-29). Where a test used GPT-6 Luna via the ordinary chat/Responses API rather than the Decisions endpoint, it is labelled "Luna (chat)" and is not treated as a Decisions-API result. Affiliation flags: **[V]** = author/publisher has a commercial stake in one of the compared products or in a competing product; **[I]** = individual or firm with no disclosed stake. All dates are 2026.

Source inventory actually examined (13 primary pages): vincentvandeth.nl (I), agent.nexus (V, uses Jev), respan.ai (V, sells Span decision models), WallerChen/jev-measured GitHub (I), evaboot.com (V, lead-gen vendor), stacktoheap.com (I), arize.com (V, eval-tool vendor), btinsights.ai (V, survey-coding vendor), querypanel.io (V, Jev-based pipeline), hightouch.com (V, identity-resolution vendor), Artificial Analysis v1 + v2 (independent benchmark org), aimultiple.com AIM System One Index (independent; states no vendor relationship), pinggy.io roundup (V, unrelated tunnelling vendor; secondary aggregator), firecrawl.dev (V, unrelated; secondary).

## Key question 1: Across independent tests, who wins on accuracy, by how much, and on which task types?

### Takeaway
On the eight head-to-head datasets I could verify, accuracy is essentially tied: Jev wins clearly on two (lead scoring, RAGTruth hallucination detection), Decisions wins modestly on two (identity resolution easy set, survey open-end coding after threshold tuning), and the rest are within noise. The gaps that are statistically defended are all under 4 F1 points except Evaboot's lead-scoring test (Jev +25 pts exact-level) and Respan's cited Anand routing test (Jev +17.8 macro-F1), both of which come from vendor-adjacent sources.

### Inferences
- Pooling the four largest neutral-ish datasets (Nexus 2,155; BTInsights 2,617; jev-measured 633; van Deth 600) the accuracy gap is under 1 point in either direction; the only large gaps (Evaboot +25, Anand +17.8) come from ordinal-scoring or option-order-sensitivity tasks where Decisions' answer distribution seems to shift (one level stricter in Evaboot; 36% route changes across option orders in Anand).
- Decisions appears more robust on discrete entity-matching and hallucination-adjacent binary tasks when its threshold is tuned (Hightouch, BTInsights), while Jev appears more robust to option-order permutation (Anand via Respan) and to prompt wording (Arize ROC-AUC stable across both wordings for both, but Jev higher).
- Trained task-specific classifiers (MiniLM 89.87% on BANKING77) still beat both zero-shot decision models by 4–5 points on narrow intent sets, and frontier LLMs (Opus 5.5, Haiku 5.5) beat both on hard sets by 3–6 points at 10–90x the cost.

### Gaps
- No independent voice-agent decision benchmark was found; "evalgent.com" returned no results in any search. The closest proxies are QueryPanel's per-stage latency (not voice) and Hightouch/Arize latency figures.
- Could not locate the akitaonrails.com post ("Which Jev-like AI Decision/Classifier Model Is Best?", Oct 10); the suggested URL returns 404 and searches surface only Respan's secondhand citation (90.5% vs 97.6%). Dataset size, task list and methodology are therefore unknown.
- Kartik Anand's 500-case routing test (Respan's source for the 71.4 vs 89.2 macro-F1 and Brier 0.296 vs 0.117) could not be located as a primary source.
- No source isolates "RAG relevance" as a labelled task with both models; Arize's RAGTruth (hallucination) and Phoenix evaluator suites are the nearest.

## Key question 2: How do calibration quality and confidence thresholds compare?

### Takeaway
Calibration evidence is split and task-dependent: Jev has lower ECE on the van Deth (0.014 vs 0.054) and Arize (0.023 vs 0.042) tests, Decisions has lower ECE on BANKING77 (4.35% vs 9.33%), and Respan's cited Brier scores favour Jev (0.117 vs 0.296). The one consistent operational finding is that Decisions' useful threshold sits higher (70–90%) than Jev's (55–70%), and that tuning the threshold moves both models' accuracy by 3–4 points.

### Cited Findings
- 450 Dutch texts: Jev ECE 0.014 (0.015 in round 1); answers at >=90% confidence correct 98.7% of the time (471 answers). Decisions ECE 0.054; mean confidence 0.897 at 95.0% accuracy; its >=90% bucket was 415/415 correct. On the yes/no "needs a human" question Decisions had AUC 0.996 but only 84% accuracy at a 0.5 threshold (24 of 77 no-escalation emails scored >=0.5); a post-hoc 0.95 threshold gave 97%. Haiku self-reported confidence ECE 0.112 [I] — [vincentvandeth.nl](https://vincentvandeth.nl/blog/jev-vs-openai-decisions-api-vs-haiku-5-5-benchmark)
- BANKING77 (10-bin ECE, refusals excluded): definitions-only Decisions ECE 4.35% / Brier 0.3163 vs Jev 9.33% / 0.3078; with 2 examples per intent Decisions 4.63% / 0.2472 vs Jev 6.50% / 0.2206. So Decisions is better on ECE, Jev slightly better on Brier, in both conditions [I] — [stacktoheap](https://stacktoheap.com/blog/2026/10/08/jev-the-decisions-strike-back/)
- Arize "calibration error" (mean gap between stated confidence and accuracy on 655 evaluator cases): Jev 0.023; Decisions 0.042; Liquid d1 0.051; Kev 9B 0.055 [V] — [Arize](https://arize.com/blog/decision-model-benchmark.md)
- Respan cites Brier 0.296 (Decisions) vs 0.117 (Jev) without naming the producing test (likely Anand's 500 cases) [V] — [Respan](https://www.respan.ai/articles/openai-decisions-api-vs-jev)
- jev-measured, "wrong at >=90% confidence" on choice tasks: Jev 11.5%; Decisions 9.2%; Clef 2.3%; Kev 4B 2.6%; d1 3.2%; Decider 4.6%; Solar Decide 13.7%; Laya 26.8%. Jev confidence on ticket set: 0.979 clear / 0.841 ambiguous [I] — [WallerChen/jev-measured](https://github.com/WallerChen/jev-measured)
- Artificial Analysis v1 (Oct 13): decision models as a class are overconfident on HLE-MultiChoice and AA-Omniscience ("accuracy falls below stated probabilities at almost every level"), but mostly well calibrated on When2Call and WildGuardTest; no numeric ECE given — [Artificial Analysis v1](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-decision-index)
- Artificial Analysis v2 (Nov 8) makes calibration part of the headline metric: Decision Score = 50 x (1 + Accuracy - Brier). Brier figures given only for Clef (0.27) and Celeris-1.1 Decision (0.15); none published for Jev 1.14 or Decisions in the article — [Artificial Analysis v2](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/decision-index-v2)
- JevBench (Benchmark Heaven, Oct 16) gives Decisions a "calibration" sub-score of 90; no Jev calibration sub-score reported in the roundup [secondary, V] — [pinggy.io roundup](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_ai_decision_models/)
- Operating thresholds chosen by testers: Hightouch Jev 60% / Decisions 70% (raising Jev's cutoff from 50 to 60% changed accuracy 95.6→95.7%; Luna unchanged at 97.3%) [V] — [Hightouch](https://hightouch.com/blog/llm-judges-identity-resolution); BTInsights Jev 55–85% / Decisions 70–90%, with adjusted thresholds adding +3.2 (Jev) and +4.2 (Decisions) F1 and shifting precision/recall from 65/81 → 76/75 (Jev) and 65/82 → 79/76 (Decisions) [V] — [BTInsights](https://btinsights.ai/blog/jev-vs-openai-decisions-api-coding-accuracy/)
- Determinism as a proxy for probability stability: Decisions returned identical scores on all 998 leads across 3 runs; Jev changed ~10% of leads by one level (mean spread 0.03) [V] — [Evaboot](https://evaboot.com/blog/openai-decisions-api-vs-jev). Jev gave the same answer on 594/600 category questions 13 days apart [I] — [vincentvandeth.nl](https://vincentvandeth.nl/blog/jev-vs-openai-decisions-api-vs-haiku-5-5-benchmark). Respan/Anand: Decisions identical probabilities on 498/500 reruns vs Jev 95.2% same route [V] — [Respan](https://www.respan.ai/articles/openai-decisions-api-vs-jev)

### Inferences
- The ECE disagreement (van Deth/Arize favour Jev; stacktoheap favours Decisions) tracks option count: Jev's ECE is worse on the 77-way BANKING77 task where its "wrong at >=90%" rate is also high (11.5% in jev-measured), while on 2–6-option tasks Jev's ECE is very low. Decisions' ECE is more stable across option counts but its probabilities skew conservative/strict, which is why testers land on 70–90% cutoffs.
- Decisions' near-perfect run-to-run determinism suggests near-greedy decoding; Jev's ~5–10% flip rate at the margin implies sampling or non-deterministic serving, which matters for threshold-based routing rules.

### Gaps
- No source reports ECE or Brier for both models on a many-option task AND a binary task from the same harness, so the "option count drives calibration" inference is cross-source.
- No per-region or per-payload-size calibration data exists.
- Artificial Analysis v2 does not publish Brier for Jev 1.14 or GPT-6 Luna (Decisions) in its article; the live leaderboard may.

### Inferences
- Median rankings flip with gateway: tests calling Jev through OpenRouter (van Deth, jev-measured iad1, Respan) find Decisions faster; tests calling TypeSafe directly or with short payloads (Arize, Hightouch, Nexus, QueryPanel intent) find Jev equal or faster. jev-measured's direct-vs-OpenRouter p50 gap (313 vs 734 ms) is large enough to explain most of the disagreement.
- Decisions' tail (p99 2.8x Jev's in Nexus) and cold-start (875 ms first call in QueryPanel) are the clearest latency liabilities for real-time/voice use; Jev's liability is a single-region US-West endpoint for non-US callers.
- AIM's much higher Jev median (574 ms) versus Artificial Analysis (92–130 ms) likely reflects AIM's longer browser-task payloads versus AA's short questions; the two indexes are not comparable on latency.

### Gaps
- No published p95 for both models from any non-US region. The only non-US tester (van Deth, Netherlands) did not state call origin and used OpenRouter.
- stacktoheap reports Jev means only (no p95) and from historical runs on different dates than Decisions.
- No voice-agent end-to-end turn latency with either model was found.

## Key question 4: Measured cost per million decisions under realistic payloads, and does OpenAI's few-shot support change the accuracy picture?

### Takeaway
At list prices ($0.042 vs $0.10 per 1M input tokens, output free on both) Jev costs 1.8–2.4x less per decision in every head-to-head that measured tokens, landing at roughly $11–105 per million decisions versus $24–224 for Decisions depending on payload (500–2,500 input tokens). Few-shot examples lift Decisions by ~5 points on BANKING77 but Jev gains about the same from the same examples, so the gap does not close; examples also raise Decisions' token bill 2.9x and mean latency 35%.

### Inferences
- Realistic cost-per-million scales linearly with payload: ~$30–40/M at ~500–800 tokens (jev-measured, Evaboot) up to ~$105–224/M at ~2,200–2,500 tokens (Nexus). Jev's higher per-call fixed overhead (~260 tokens) means its ratio advantage shrinks on very short payloads (1.3x in jev-measured) and approaches the 2.4x list-price ratio on long ones.
- Few-shot does not change the winner: both models gain ~4–5 points from the same examples on BANKING77, and the cost of carrying 154 examples per call triples Decisions' bill, which erodes its only structural pricing path (per-question rather than per-call overhead).
- Multi-question payloads favour Decisions' overhead model (per-question ~150 tokens) only when asking one or two questions; Nexus's eight-question workload still came out 2.1x costlier on Decisions.

### Gaps
- No tester measured cost under image payloads (Decisions-only feature) or at the 32K Jev ceiling.
- Decisions' pricing was reported as "not published" by Firecrawl (Sept 30) yet every October tester used $0.10/1M; I did not fetch OpenAI's pricing page to confirm the current Decisions-specific rate.
- No independent few-shot experiment other than stacktoheap's single BANKING77 run; no test of prompt decomposition (one multi-option question vs several yes/no questions) comparing both models on accuracy.

## Where results conflict and why

### Takeaway
Nearly every disagreement between tests maps to one of four variables: gateway/region (OpenRouter vs direct, US vs unstated), payload size (500 vs 2,500 tokens), option count (binary vs 59–77-way), or prompt/threshold style (default 50% vs tuned 70–90%, examples vs definitions). Vendor affiliation correlates with direction: the two largest pro-Jev gaps come from Jev users/partners, the two pro-Decisions results come from vendors whose products wrap either model.

### Cited Findings
- Gateway effect on Jev latency: direct api.typesafe.ai p50 313 ms / p90 423 ms vs OpenRouter p50 734 ms / p90 1,739 ms in interleaved runs; OpenRouter from iad1 184 ms — [WallerChen/jev-measured](https://github.com/WallerChen/jev-measured). Tests that found Decisions faster all used OpenRouter for Jev — [vincentvandeth.nl](https://vincentvandeth.nl/blog/jev-vs-openai-decisions-api-vs-haiku-5-5-benchmark), [Respan](https://www.respan.ai/articles/openai-decisions-api-vs-jev)
- Option-order sensitivity: Decisions same route across six option orders 64.2% vs Jev 91.6%; macro-F1 range for Decisions 60.6–86.6% by order [V] — [Respan](https://www.respan.ai/articles/openai-decisions-api-vs-jev)
- Question-wording sensitivity: Liquid d1 ROC-AUC fell from 0.92 to 0.17 on the "unsupported claim" phrasing; Jev and Decisions stable (0.93/0.93 and 0.90/0.89) [V] — [Arize](https://arize.com/blog/decision-model-benchmark.md)
- Threshold tuning swings: +3–4 F1 for both models (BTInsights); 46%→64% (Decisions) and 71%→73% (Jev) exact-level on leads with optimistic in-sample tuning (Evaboot); 84%→97% for Decisions on van Deth's yes/no question moving the threshold from 0.5 to 0.95 — [BTInsights](https://btinsights.ai/blog/jev-vs-openai-decisions-api-coding-accuracy/), [Evaboot](https://evaboot.com/blog/openai-decisions-api-vs-jev), [vincentvandeth.nl](https://vincentvandeth.nl/blog/jev-vs-openai-decisions-api-vs-haiku-5-5-benchmark)
- Thresholds tuned on one model and not the other: Nexus tuned on Jev and did not retune for Decisions, which plausibly inflates Decisions' error rate [V] — [agent.nexus](https://agent.nexus/blog/openai-decisions-api-vs-jev)
- Decisions in limited preview until Oct 6 then public beta; several Sept 30–Oct 7 tests therefore ran on preview-period infrastructure — [WallerChen/jev-measured](https://github.com/WallerChen/jev-measured), [Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/blog/openai-decisions-api-vs-jev)
- Model naming inconsistency across sources ("GPT-5.6 Luna" vs "GPT-6 Luna"; "GPT-6 Luna (Low)" chat vs "GPT-6 Luna (Decisions)") makes some leaderboard rows ambiguous — [AA v1](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-decision-index)

### Inferences
- The only robust cross-test conclusions are: (1) Jev costs ~2x less per decision at equal payload; (2) Decisions has a heavier latency tail and cold start; (3) accuracy is a coin-flip on most tasks and should be decided per task with the tester's own labelled data, which is what every independent author recommends.
- Readers should discount the Evaboot (+25) and Anand (+17.8) gaps until replicated: both derive from ordinal/route tasks where Decisions' systematically stricter scoring can be partly corrected by threshold choice.

### Gaps
- No test varied payload size or option count within one harness for both models, so attributions of conflict to those variables are inferred from cross-source comparison, not measured.
- No test compared Decisions' per-question overhead model vs Jev's per-call overhead at 1, 4 and 8 questions with identical content (Nexus gives figures but its own numbers are internally inconsistent).
