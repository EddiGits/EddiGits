# TypeSafe Jev as of mid-November 2026, and the decision-model entrants competing with it and with OpenAI's Decisions API

Research date: 14 November 2026. "Vendor claim" = figure published by the vendor itself; "independent" = third party ran the test. Where only secondary blogs exist, this is flagged. TypeSafe's own blog listing page carried no post dates, and several primary pages (Dealroom, Forbes) returned 403/429, so some TypeSafe-voice material is thin.

## Key Question 1: What does Jev cost and offer today, and how has it changed since September 2026?

### Takeaway
As of 12-14 November 2026 Jev still costs exactly its launch price of $0.042 per million input tokens with free output; TypeSafe has published no price change, no subsidy statement, and no batch/fine-tuning/EU-region product. What did change: Jev 1.14 (14 Oct, "small, subtle improvements") became `jev-latest` around 24 Oct; three opt-in "Danger Zone" features (PNG/JPEG images, a new Box bounding-box question type, 262k context) shipped; rate limits were cut on 30 Sep and then un-published on 14 Oct; direct signups reopened 27 Sep without free credits; and TypeSafe raised an $870M Series A at $7.5B on 9 Oct. There is no evidence of a jev-2; "jev-router" exists only as an OpenRouter listing.

**New features (all opt-in "Danger Zone", not production-ready per TypeSafe)**
- Images: header `TypeSafe-Danger-Zone-Images: true`; PNG/JPEG stills only, up to 16 MiB each, 256-1568 px per side, 32 MiB per request; works on 1.13 and 1.14; each image reference is billed as input tokens; missing header returns 400. Image performance "still trails" text. — [systemonemodels.org](https://systemonemodels.org/guides/jev-1-14/)
- Box: new question type returning a bounding box (top/bottom/left/right as 0-1 fractions plus confidence); requires 1.14 and the images header; has no "not found" answer so docs suggest pairing with a Noul. — [systemonemodels.org](https://systemonemodels.org/guides/jev-1-14/); [MarkTechPost 14 Oct](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/14/typesafe-ai-jev-1-14-images-box-long-context/)
- Long context: header `TypeSafe-Danger-Zone-Long-Context: true` raises per-request (default 64k) and per-question (default 32k) limits to 262k tokens, "the same per token as any other request" but "slower and less accurate." — [docs.typesafe.ai/models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models) (primary)
- Structured instructions/criteria (objects or arrays) added 20 Sep; Choice capped at 255 options. — [mrjev.com changelog](https://mrjev.com/changelog/)
- SDKs: Python 0.7.5 / JS 0.7.0 (14 Oct) added image support and default zstd compression of bodies >= 1 KiB; JS SDK requires Node 22+. — [mrjev.com changelog](https://mrjev.com/changelog/)
- Opt-in data sharing (14 Oct): users may let TypeSafe use their queries to find model weaknesses; shared queries will not be used for training. — [mrjev.com changelog](https://mrjev.com/changelog/); [systemonemodels.org](https://systemonemodels.org/guides/jev-1-14/)
- Not offered: no context caching, no batch API, no fine-tuning, no few-shot-examples feature, no EU region appear in TypeSafe docs or any tracker. Jev is US-hosted (West Coast) with no EU/UK/Canada residency. — [docs.typesafe.ai/models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models); [colchix EU readiness](https://colchix.com/blog/can-european-enterprises-use-jev) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Price stability through two rival launches (OpenAI 6 Oct beta, Google 11 Nov) plus a claimed-profitable business and a $7.5B raise suggests TypeSafe is not currently under pressure to cut below $0.042; the Flowtivity "cannot rule out subsidy" line remains the only hint otherwise and is unsourced to TypeSafe wording.
- The 30 Sep rate-limit cut and the later un-publishing of limits are consistent with capacity constraint rather than demand weakness (1T tokens/day claim; "System overload" incident 30 Oct).
- jev-router appearing only on OpenRouter, with no TypeSafe docs, is more likely an OpenRouter-side routing construct than a TypeSafe product.

### Gaps
- No evidence of jev-2, fine-tuning, batch API, few-shot examples, or an EU region; TypeSafe has announced none (absence confirmed on docs.typesafe.ai/models and trackers, 14 Nov 2026).
- No TypeSafe statement on whether $0.042 is subsidized; the only subsidy mention is a secondary review's paraphrase.
- Current numeric rate limits are in the console only, not public.
- TypeSafe's blog posts carry no visible dates on the index page, so exact publication dates of its own posts could not be confirmed.

## Key Question 2: How do Gemini Decision 1 and Microsoft Decision-1 compare on price and claimed performance with Jev and OpenAI (plus Laya and other entrants)?

### Takeaway
Price tiers as of 14 Nov 2026: OpenAI Decisions (GPT-6 Luna) and Gemini Decision 1 both $0.10/M input (OpenAI adds $0.01/M cached); Microsoft-Decision-1 and Jev both $0.042; Cloudflare Clef-flash $0.038; several smaller hosted entrants (Nace Drex, Bespoke Nimble, Celeris, Cicero, Liquid) undercut all of them at $0.004-$0.05; Laya and other open-weight models are free to self-host. On performance, every vendor claims a lead on its own suite (Google: 92.2% JevBench, 7 of 9 benchmarks; Microsoft: 83.5% avg across 36 benchmarks vs Jev 82.3%), but the only independent cross-vendor index, Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1 (11 Nov), scores Gemini Decision 1 at 66, tied with Jev 1.14 (9th of 18), behind Drex Max v1.1 (72.6); Microsoft-Decision-1 scores ~62 there. Nobody has reproduced Google's launch numbers.

### Cited Findings

**Google Gemini Decisions API / Gemini Decision 1 (launched Wed 11 Nov 2026)**
- Price: $0.10 per 1M input tokens, output free ("generates zero text tokens, so output token usage is always 0"); shared input billed once per request regardless of question count; free tier for prototyping in AI Studio (free-tier data may be used to improve products; paid tier is not); no context caching, Batch API or Flex tier. — [eesel.ai Gemini Decisions](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gemini-decisions-api) (secondary, figures checked 12 Nov); [VentureBeat 11 Nov](https://venturebeat.com/technology/googles-gemini-decision-1-model-scores-up-to-1-000-questions-in-one-api-call)
- Free tier limits: 200 requests and 50,000 input tokens per minute. Example: one shared-input request ~ $0.50 per 1,000 records vs ~$8.10 for 20 separate calls. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/googles-gemini-decision-1-model-scores-up-to-1-000-questions-in-one-api-call)
- Inputs: text, images, audio, video and PDF mixed in one request (the main differentiator); 262,144-token context; 1-1,000 questions per request; Choice 2-255 options; Score 2-255 levels (2-10 recommended). — [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gemini-decisions-api); [MarkTechPost 11 Nov](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/11/google-gemini-decisions-api-gemini-decision-1/)
- Latency (vendor claim): 150-250 ms end-to-end; median model compute 50-100 ms for inputs up to 16K tokens. — [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gemini-decisions-api)
- Availability: Gemini Developer API (v1beta) and OpenRouter; not on Vertex AI as of 12 Nov, Google Cloud support "coming soon." — [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gemini-decisions-api)
- Google benchmark claims (vendor-run): JevBench 92.2% vs GPT-6 Luna 90.9% vs Jev 1.14 90.0%; Mind2Web Multimodal Jev 86.8% vs Decision 1 86.2%; When2Call Jev 82.0% vs Decision 1 79.9%; MMAU 71.8% vs Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite 67.7%; Video-MME 69.1% vs 63.3%; calibration error 0.0250 vs Jev 0.0494; shared test cost $0.88 (Decision 1) vs $0.68 (Jev) vs $4.14 (Flash-Lite). Google says Decision 1 "leads across the suite, with the lowest calibration error" and is "nearly 5x cheaper" than Flash-Lite. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/googles-gemini-decision-1-model-scores-up-to-1-000-questions-in-one-api-call); [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gemini-decisions-api); "tops 7 of 9 benchmarks" per [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/4065770698331011)
- Independent reproduction: none. eesel states the results "hasn't been reproduced independently yet"; VentureBeat and HN cite no third-party reruns of Google's suite. — [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gemini-decisions-api); [HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50456791)
- Independent index: Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1 scores Gemini Decision 1 at 66 (65.98), 9th of 18, tied with Jev 1.14 and Elephant 9B; leaders Drex Max v1.1 and GLiDE v1.2 at ~72.6; AA-Paperclip all-answers-correct 33% (Gemini) vs 35.5% (Jev) vs 63% (Drex Max); AA When2Call Drex Max 94.6%, Jev 80.8%, Gemini 75.2%; Gemini is among the pricier models on the index at $345 per 1M tasks (4th-highest of 18). — [Artificial Analysis on X](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2120582049522127205); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/googles-gemini-decision-1-model-scores-up-to-1-000-questions-in-one-api-call); [OrcaRouter](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/gemini-decision-1-release-decision-index) (secondary)
- Hacker News (~160 comments, ~12 Nov): one user reports Gemini at ~4x lower Brier than Jev (2x-6x by task), p50 latency >100 ms lower, but ~2x end-to-end cost; another says Jev is still faster/cheaper for common text uses; commenters call $0.10 "very overpriced" vs Liquid at $0.02/M; multimodal input seen as the real differentiator; general scepticism of vendor benchmarks. — [HN: Gemini Decisions API](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50456791) (anecdotal)

**Microsoft-Decision-1 (launched 9 Oct 2026)**
- What it is: a decision-scoring model that returns probabilities over fixed options, no text or rationales; Microsoft post-trained Alibaba's open-weight Qwen3.5-9B and says it will "soon rebase it on other models" (reported as MAI and OpenAI models); weights not released. — [eesel.ai Microsoft-Decision-1 pricing](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/microsoft-decision-1-pricing); [MarkTechPost 9 Oct](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/09/microsoft-ai-releases-microsoft-decision-1-a-qwen3-5-9b-decision-scoring-model/); [Neomanex](https://neomanex.com/news/microsoft-decision-1-decision-model-foundry-openrouter-0-042)
- Price: $0.042 per million input tokens, output free (Microsoft launch post); OpenRouter same rate (weighted average paid $0.04152 in first days); no batch, cached-input or provisioned-throughput rates; not on Azure's pricing page as of 11 Oct. — [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/microsoft-decision-1-pricing)
- Where it runs: Microsoft Foundry (billed via Azure, "Direct from Azure", GA version 1) and OpenRouter (served by Azure); missing from Microsoft's region availability page; regional rates and rate limits undocumented; 32,768-token context; text-in/JSON-out; yes/no, multiple choice, rating, rubric questions. — [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/microsoft-decision-1-pricing)
- Microsoft benchmark claims (vendor-run): 36 benchmarks, 147,137 questions, 83.5% average accuracy vs 82.3% Jev 1.13.0 vs 79.4% GPT-6 Luna Decisions; calibration 92.2 (third) vs Jev 93.7; median latency 85 ms (p95 125 ms) via Foundry same-region vs Jev 240 ms and Luna 300 ms; internal claims that Copilot found it "competitive with GPT5.6 Luna and 100 times faster" and Xbox Research "200 times less expensive" than GPT-6 Sol; cost comparison only vs GPT-6 Sol (~$11 vs ~$2,434 per million texts). — [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/microsoft-decision-1-pricing); [AIToolsReview](https://aitoolsreview.co.uk/insights/microsoft-decision-1-fast-decision-model)
- Independent observations: OpenRouter p50 0.19 s including network; not on the community Jev Decision Index leaderboard; one HN tester found it ~0.72x Jev's cost on a small private suite; a commenter: "if you need calibration better use JEV." Artificial Analysis v2.1 scores it ~62 vs Gemini's 66. — [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/microsoft-decision-1-pricing); [OrcaRouter Gemini vs Microsoft](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/gemini-decision-1-vs-microsoft-decision-1) (secondary)

**OpenAI Decisions API (reference point)**
- Public beta 6 Oct 2026 on GPT-6 Luna; now GA per OpenAI's announcement; $0.10/M input, $0.01/M cached input, no output charge; up to 400 questions per request. — [OpenAI announcement](https://openai.com/index/decisions-api/) (primary, fetch returned 403; pricing confirmed via [Munder Difflin](https://munderdiffl.in/blog/openai-decisions-api/) and [awesome-system-one](https://github.com/andyrewlee/awesome-system-one)); beta date per [WorkerKit](https://workerkit.ai/blog/openai-decisions-api-vs-jev)
- OpenAI claims ~150 ms vs ~1.6 s for GPT-6 Luna chat. — [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/openai-decision-api-luna/)
- Independent: an open-ends coding benchmark found Jev and Decisions "about equally accurate at default strictness," with Decisions ~7x the cost. — [BT Insights](https://btinsights.ai/blog/jev-vs-openai-decisions-api-coding-accuracy/); Parallel's 231-question JevBench run: Decisions 90.0%, Parallel 89.6%, Jev 88.3% (vendor-run by Parallel). — [Parallel](https://parallel.ai/articles/laya-vs-jev-vs-parallel); HN testers report Decisions ~2x slower, less accurate and pricier than Jev. — [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50026075)

## Key Question 4: Which vendor offers the best enterprise terms (data retention, regions, SLAs)?

### Takeaway
On paper Microsoft (Azure SLAs, Microsoft support, GA status) and OpenAI (GA, published ZDR eligibility for /v1/decisions, EU regional processing via ZDR, HIPAA BAA path) have the most mature enterprise frameworks; Google's paid tier excludes data from product improvement but Decision 1 is still v1beta, not on Vertex, with no stated SLA or region; TypeSafe does not train on customer data and offers ZDR to enterprise customers on request, but has no EU region, no published retention period, no published SLA and no formal deprecation policy. Open-weight options (Laya, Clef, Strands Decider) are the only route to full data sovereignty. Evidence is thin for Microsoft's and Google's decision-specific terms.

### Cited Findings
- **TypeSafe/Jev**: "Jev is not trained on customer requests or responses"; ZDR referenced for enterprise customers via the Legal page; no retention period stated; no regions stated. — [docs.typesafe.ai/models](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models) (primary). Status page shows regional endpoints api.us-east-1, us-east-2, us-west-2 and api.us (US only). — [status.typesafe.ai](https://status.typesafe.ai). DPA (updated 24 Apr 2026) incorporates EU SCCs Module 2/3, names Ireland as EEA supervisory authority, commits to 72-hour incident notice; default retention "as long as reasonably necessary"; no EU/UK/Canada residency; enterprise page says higher limits and ZDR "available to companies on request." — [colchix](https://colchix.com/blog/can-european-enterprises-use-jev); [jev101 privacy guide](https://jev101.org/guides/jev-privacy-guide); [vendortrustindex](https://vendortrustindex.com/vendors/jev) (secondary, several from EU-hosting vendors). Both Jev endpoints are on OpenRouter's ZDR list so ZDR can be enforced per request, but Jev is not available on OpenRouter's EU in-region routing. — [HostYourAI](https://hostyourai.com/en/jev) (vendor of EU alternatives). ### Inferences
- For regulated EU buyers, no hosted decision API currently offers in-region processing by default: OpenAI gets closest (EU regional processing conditional on ZDR approval); Jev and Microsoft are US/undocumented; Google is beta with Cloud "coming soon." Self-hosting Laya/Clef/Strands is the only clean answer today.
- Microsoft's Azure wrapper gives it the strongest contractual story (SLA, support, Azure billing) despite the thinnest product documentation; TypeSafe's terms are the most "startup-shaped" (on-request ZDR, no SLA, no retention number).

### Gaps
- No published SLA or uptime commitment found for Jev, Gemini Decision 1 or OpenAI Decisions; only TypeSafe publishes a status page with uptime figures (periods unlabelled).
- Microsoft-Decision-1's data-retention terms and Google's Decision-1-specific enterprise terms (Vertex ETA, regions) are not stated anywhere retrieved; verify directly with vendor pages.
- Several EU/privacy sources are from vendors selling EU-hosted alternatives; their framing of Jev's gaps may be self-serving.
